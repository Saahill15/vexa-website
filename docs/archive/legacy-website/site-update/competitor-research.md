# VEXA competitor and related website research

Prepared 24 September 2026 for VEXA (https://www.vexadigital.in), a Delhi based gaming and esports commercial architecture business run by Soda (Kaustubh), trading under Blue Wings Esports.

Scope of this file: 10 competitors and 10 related websites, each opened and studied. Own site audit and buyer search behaviour are covered by other researchers and are not repeated here.

## How to read this file

Every finding carries an evidence label so you know how much to trust it.

* OBSERVED means I watched it happen in Claude in Chrome (screenshots at load, after a few seconds, and after scrolling, plus JavaScript probes of the live page for fonts, libraries, videos and canvases).
* SOURCE means I read it from the page HTML, the linked stylesheets, or the text of the page fetched directly. Library names, colours, fonts and keyframe names come from here.
* INFERRED means I could not watch it, so I am reasoning from source. These are flagged wherever they appear.

Chrome limits you should know about. The browser window was a narrow portrait size for the first part of the session and a normal desktop size later, so several sites were seen on a phone sized viewport and others on desktop. Where a site was only seen in one of the two, the other is marked INFERRED from CSS breakpoints. Chrome tabs opened with the new tab command dropped out of the tool group, so I studied the top sites in the one permitted tab, one after another, instead of six parallel tabs. Details are in the access log at the end.

Nothing here is legal advice. Section C flags Indian legal context for VEXA to check with a lawyer or company secretary.

## 1. AOG India Esports (Alliance Of Gamers)

**URL.** https://aogindia.com (also /for-brands.html and /for-agencies.html)

**What they are.** A gaming creator network and campaign execution agency with a stated network of 200 plus creators across BGMI, Free Fire, Valorant, Minecraft and GTA V on YouTube and Instagram.

**Sells to.** Brand agencies, media buyers, PR agencies and corporate brand marketers (as a white label execution arm), plus gaming, tech and Gen Z brands directly. It also runs a title sponsorship offer.

**Positioning and headline.** Visible hero is "ALLIANCE OF GAMERS" with the strap "200+ CREATORS. MOBILE & PC GAMING, TECH & GEN Z." The page H1 (built for search) is "Gaming Marketing Agency India | Gaming Influencer & Media Agency". The positioning sentence is "specialist white-label creator execution arm for Brand Agencies, Media Buyers, PR Agencies". [OBSERVED and SOURCE]

**Page structure (homepage order).** Sticky nav (Home, About, Services, Creators, Work With Us menu with For Brands and For Agencies, Contact). Hero. Who We Are and How Campaigns Work (short paragraph with a "see more" expander and a four step flow: Creator Content, Unique Link and UTM, Landing Page, Download or Action). Strategic Partnership Opportunity card (title sponsor pitch). Creators grid with subscriber and follower counts. Brands We've Worked With (nine tiles). Stats strip. Four service cards. FAQ accordion (five questions). Enquiry form. Footer with three role based email addresses. [SOURCE]

**Proof and trust.** Numbers: 200+ network, 1M+ combined subscribers, 3M+ monthly views, 30K+ average views per upload, and "5M+ Guaranteed Monthly Views" for the sponsorship package. Six creator cards with live subscriber counts (for example FaceMe Gaming 575K). Brand tiles: Rooter, 1Win, 1xBet, SpinBot, Save8 AI, FlovaAI, Bajaj Insta EMI, Last Pirates, BonusBuddy. No case studies with results, no testimonials, no named team, no privacy or terms page (the footer holds only a copyright line). Weakness: two of the brand tiles (1Win, 1xBet) are betting or real money brands, which is a live legal risk in India (see Section C). [SOURCE]

**CTA and pricing.** Primary CTA "WORK WITH US" (slanted orange button). Secondary CTA "APPLY FOR TITLE SPONSORSHIP" with scarcity copy "Limited to 1 Exclusive Title Brand Partner for 2026." Pricing is public in the FAQ: product seeding from ₹50,000 to ₹1,00,000, mid tier BGMI or YouTube campaigns ₹2,00,000 to ₹5,00,000, and "Multi-creator campaigns exceed ₹10,00,000". The form asks for budget in four bands (under ₹50,000, ₹50,000 to ₹2,00,000, ₹2,00,000 to ₹5,00,000, above ₹5,00,000). Promises a reply in one to two business days. [SOURCE]

The FAQ even names rival agencies (Grynow Media, Chtrbox, Monk Entertainment). Short, punchy, uppercase labels. No stories. [SOURCE]

**Visual system.**

* Loading or preloader: none observed. The page appears immediately. [OBSERVED]
* Scroll and hover motion: AOS (Animate On Scroll library from unpkg) fade up reveals; headings arrive dim then light up. Keyframes for marquees, a pulsing crosshair, a badge pulse, glowing logo and tada style hover. No GSAP, Lenis, Lottie or Three found by probe. [OBSERVED and SOURCE]
* Graphics and imagery: creator portrait photography; a process diagram with dotted path and icons; boxicons icon set; dragon logo with glow.
* Image placement: full bleed hero, creator cards in grid, brand tiles in grid.
* Video: none on the page. [OBSERVED]

# PART 3. THE THREE ANALYSIS SECTIONS

## Section A. Top 5 competitor profiles, ranked by how well they sell

Ranking method: how clearly the site tells a buyer what happens, proves it with evidence, and lowers the friction to a first conversation. Design polish is a secondary factor.

### Rank 1. Fourth Floor Creative

Why it ranks first: it combines a logo wall, named testimonials, awards, and case studies with numbers, then adds pages that help a procurement team say yes (New Supplier, Resources and Forms, Glossary).

Model these things for VEXA, named specifically:
* **Proof strip layout.** A row of white brand marks on black in four columns on mobile and eight on desktop, sitting directly under the award badges. For VEXA, use only real clients and mark permission status internally.
* **Case study format.** Overview, The Challenge, Our Approach, Results, with the results line in one sentence containing three numbers. Sample skeleton from the Apex case: "400 pieces of content across a four week event… 45,000 peak viewers… 37M views".
* **Testimonial trio.** Three short quotes with the brand name only as the attribution, each praising a behaviour (speed, clarity, agility).
* **Buyer support pages.** A "New Supplier" page (company details, GST, PAN, bank letter checklist, vendor form) and a "Resources and Forms" page. For Indian brands this replaces three emails of back and forth.
* **Glossary page.** A short glossary of deal terms (usage rights, exclusivity window, whitelisting, CPV, EMV) which also earns search traffic.
* **Hero type contrast.** A heavy uppercase line followed by a very large condensed light line (their "SUPERPOWERED DATA"). Cheap, striking, works without images.
* **Legal footer.** Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy links, office locations, entity name.
* **Do not copy.** The full width cookie banner that covers a third of a phone screen; use a compact bar.

### Rank 2. Gamesight

Why: the whole company is explained in one scroll with three claims that carry weight: scale of games served, dollars managed and a named client quote with a cost result.

* **Legal and cookie set.** Privacy Policy, a GDPR and CCPA page, a cookie card with Reject and Accept at equal visual weight.

VEXA version: a black screen, the word VEXA decoding from random characters over about 1.2 seconds with a 000 to 100 counter, a wipe into the hero. Play it once per session (session storage), skip on return visits, and respect the reduced motion setting. Keep total block time under 1.5 seconds so it does not hurt search or mobile users.

## Section B. Why their written content is more appealing than a typical thin agency site

A typical thin agency site says: "We are a full service gaming marketing agency. We connect brands with creators and deliver results." The patterns below show what the better sites do instead. Quotes are verbatim and short. Rewrites use square brackets where VEXA must fill in a true fact.

1. **They say exactly who they sell to.**
   Quote: AOG calls itself a "specialist white-label creator execution arm for Brand Agencies, Media Buyers, PR Agencies".
   Before: "We help brands succeed in gaming."
   After: "We structure creator, team and sponsorship deals for brands that want India's 16 to 28 gaming audience."

2. **They put a number where an adjective would go.**
   Quote: Fourth Floor reports "400 pieces of content across a four week event".
   Before: "We create engaging campaigns that drive great engagement."
   After: "[N] deliverables across [N] weeks, [N] peak concurrent viewers, [N] total views."

3. **They tell the case study as Challenge, Approach, Results, not as a mood.**
   Quote: ARKA's OANDA case: "brand awareness surged by 100%" in three years, and the deal was renewed.
   Before: "A successful partnership with a leading brand."
   After: "Brief: [one sentence]. Structure: [rights, price, term]. Result: [number], renewed [n] times."

4. **They give every tactic a commercial job.**
   Quote: Ampverse labels a tactic "Role in the funnel: Trust, legitimacy, brand authority."
   Before: "We run live streams and PR."
   After: "Live stream party. Role in the campaign: launch day attention, real time conversion."

5. **They put a real limit next to the call to action.**
   Quote: "Limited to 1 Exclusive Title Brand Partner for 2026."
   Before: "Contact us to learn about sponsorship."
   After: "One exclusive [category] partner for [season]. Enquiries close [date]." (only if true)

6. **They publish prices or bands, or at least ask for a budget.**
   Quote: AOG: "Multi-creator campaigns exceed ₹10,00,000".
   Before: "Contact us for a quote."
   After: "Creator seeding from ₹[x]. Structured deals from ₹[y]. Full campaign design from ₹[z]. Send your brief and budget band."

7. **They show a point of view, not a service list.**
   Quotes: ARKA headline "The Real ROI of Sponsorship Is Not Reach"; Gamesight "Everyone claims to be data-driven. We actually are, and we can prove it."
   Before: "Why choose us? We are passionate about gaming."
   After: "Most creator deals fail on rights and payment terms, not on reach. Here is what we fix first."

8. **They use the audience's own language and metaphors.**
   Quotes: Fourth Floor, "Mix and match our services to create your perfect campaign loadout"; Skyesports, "Built for Pros. Powered by Fans."; Revox, "You focus on the game. We handle the business."
   Before: "Comprehensive marketing solutions tailored to you."
   After: "Pick your loadout: creator, team, event. We handle the contract, the brief and the payout."

9. **They praise behaviour in testimonials.**
   Quote: Fourth Floor client: "fast and clear with their communication throughout the campaign".
   Before: "Great agency, would recommend."
   After: A testimonial that names one behaviour, one deadline and one result, with name, title and brand.

10. **They teach, so buyers do not feel sold to.**
    Quote: Fourth Floor's glossary "help you navigate and understand any documentation in the Influencer Marketing space".
    Before: Jargon with no explanation (usage rights, whitelisting, CPV).
    After: A one page glossary, and an "India creator deal checklist" download.

11. **They state plain process steps in verbs.**
    Quote: ARKA: "We structure the proposal that matches budget and objectives".
    Before: "End to end solutions."
    After: "Week 1 we brief. Week 2 we shortlist. Week 3 we contract. Week 4 we go live."

Why this works: buyers reading a proposal want to forward a link that answers who, what, how much, how long and what proof. These sites answer those five questions on the page, and the thin sites answer none.

## Section C. Legal and trust pages the competitors carry, and what VEXA should match

Nothing here is legal advice. Take it to a lawyer or company secretary before publishing. I checked the pages listed below and used web sources for the Indian law summaries (PIB, EY India, a law firm summary of ASCI rules). Where a source was a summary rather than the statute, I say so.

### What competitors and related sites actually carry

* AOG India Esports: no privacy, terms or cookie page (copyright line only).
* NODWIN Gaming: a privacy policy that opens as a Google Drive PDF; company ownership line "owned, updated and maintained by NODWIN GAMING PRIVATE LIMITED"; copyright year 2024.
* Skyesports: Privacy Policy page (entity: Jet Skyesports Gaming Private Limited). Cites the Information Technology Act 2000 and rules made under it, and the intermediary guidelines rules; covers cookies, partner data and sponsored content messages.
* Collective Artists Network: Privacy Policy, Terms and Conditions and CSR Policy, plus entity name in the footer. The privacy policy states compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, includes a Grievance Officer with a Mumbai address, withdrawal of consent and retention sections, and Mumbai courts as exclusive jurisdiction.
* Revox Creatives: nothing visible.
* ARKA Alliance: cookie consent banner with category toggles from a GDPR cookie plugin; no policy links in the footer.
* Maverik / Basik Marketing: Terms and Conditions (laws of India, exclusive jurisdiction of Bengaluru courts), Privacy Policy, Disclaimer, and entity "Basik Marketing Pvt. Ltd." with address.
* Ampverse: Privacy Policy and Terms and Conditions (Singapore governing law).
* Gamesight: Privacy Policy, dedicated GDPR and CCPA page, cookie card with equal weight Reject and Accept.
* Fourth Floor Creative: Privacy Policy (mentions GDPR and a Data Protection Officer) and Cookie Policy, cookie banner with OK and Decline.
* ESL FACEIT Group: Imprint page and "General Terms and Conditions B2B Service" PDF.
* Dexerto: Editorial Standards and Review Policies, staff page, Advertise With Us.
* Passionfroot and GRIN: cookie banners (Passionfroot has Deny, No adjust, Ok; GRIN uses Cookiebot).
* Ads of the World: a Legal link in the footer.
* Lumikai and Orangutan: none visible, and stale copyright years (2021 and 2023).

### What VEXA should carry, in priority order

1. **Privacy Policy written for the DPDP Act.** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025 are the governing frame. From the EY India summary I fetched: the Data Protection Board provisions apply on notification, consent manager provisions after 12 months, and main duties for data fiduciaries after 18 months, which points to roughly May 2027 (a search result headline also cites May 2027). Notices must be in plain itemised language, and consent must be free, specific, informed and unconditional. Breach reporting is immediate to the affected people and the Board, with a detailed report within 72 hours. Build the policy now so it does not need rewriting.
2. **Children and age.** VEXA's audience is 16 to 28, so minors are in scope. The DPDP Rules require verifiable parental consent for a child's data and bar behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children (children means under 18 under the Act). Any creator sign up form, quiz, newsletter or giveaway that could reach a 16 or 17 year old needs an age gate and a parent path, or should be restricted to 18 plus. Do not copy the Collective Artists Network clause that treats a minor's data as consented to by registration, because that sits badly with the verifiable consent rule.
3. **Grievance Officer block.** Name, email and address, plus a response time. Collective Artists Network does this well.
4. **Terms of Use and Terms of Engagement.** Website terms (governing law India, jurisdiction courts of Delhi, a limitation of liability) and a separate standard engagement terms page or PDF for brands (scope, deliverables, usage rights, exclusivity, payment terms, GST). ESL's B2B terms PDF is the model. Consider an arbitration clause under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 as your lawyer advises.
5. **Sponsored content and disclosure policy.** VEXA structures influencer deals, so it should state that all paid creator content must carry a clear disclosure. The ASCI influencer guidelines (per the law firm summary I fetched) list labels such as #Ad, #Sponsored, #Paid or #Partnership, placed at the start of the caption and not buried after a "read more" fold; for video, a spoken disclosure early in the first seconds plus an on screen label for the sponsored segment. The Central Consumer Protection Authority can issue orders and penalties for misleading endorsements under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and the 2022 endorsement guidelines (the summary quoted penalties up to ₹10 lakh for individuals and up to ₹50 lakh for repeat cases; confirm the exact figures). Dexerto's visible Editorial Standards link is the site pattern to copy.
6. **Real money gaming and prohibited categories statement.** The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 prohibits online money games and their advertising, promotion and facilitation, and permits esports and online social games (from the PIB release I fetched). First offence advertising penalties reported: up to 2 years or ₹50 lakh, or both. AOG India lists 1Win and 1xBet as clients and ARKA's case list includes MSport (which appears to be a betting brand; confirm), so competitors are exposed. VEXA should publish a short "Categories we do not work with" line (betting, real money gaming, gambling adjacent brands, unregulated financial products) and check every creator deal against it. Also refer to ASCI's advertising rules for online gaming when writing campaign briefs.
7. **Cookie notice and consent card.** Equal weight Accept and Reject, a link to a plain cookie policy listing analytics and ad tools. Gamesight and Fourth Floor are the references. Keep the banner compact on mobile.
8. **Company identity block in the footer.** Legal entity name, registered office, CIN, GSTIN, and a line that VEXA is a trading name of the entity (and that it trades under Blue Wings Esports, if that is correct). Nodwin, Skyesports, Collective Artists Network and Basik all show the private limited company name; do it and keep the copyright year current (Nodwin 2024, Lumikai 2021 and Orangutan 2023 look abandoned).
9. **Disclaimer page.** Basik Marketing has one. State that reach and view projections are estimates, that creators own their opinions, and that case study figures are shared with client permission.
10. **IT Rules note.** If VEXA hosts user submitted content (for example a creator application form or public listings), consider the Information Technology Act 2000 and the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules 2021, which Skyesports cites, and publish a grievance route.
11. **If selling to global brands.** Add a GDPR and CCPA section like Gamesight's separate page, and an accessibility fix: do not block pinch zoom (Nodwin and Orangutan do).
12. **Do not copy.** A privacy policy as a Google Drive link (Nodwin), no policy at all (AOG, Revox), an anonymous testimonial (ARKA), and stale copyright years.
