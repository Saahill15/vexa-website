# VEXA Website Plan, vexadigital.in

Last updated: 22 September 2026. Written against SYSTEM.md, the doctrine files, the Vexa Foundation folder, voice.md, and VEXA_BRAND_SYSTEM.md (read that file first for tokens, type, and imagery rules this plan assumes). Every claim of fact below is drawn from a brain file and cited by file name. Nothing is invented: a case study, a number, a client name, or a testimonial that is not in the brain does not appear here, and every gap is written as a question rather than filled with a guess, per SYSTEM.md section 10.

The website has to answer six questions a first time visitor, specifically the buyer described in icp.md, is silently asking: what is the company, what do we do, what are we, why this name, why work with us, why not someone else. The page map below is built so each question has one clear home rather than being answered piecemeal across every page.

## 2. Per page purpose, sections, and copy

Copy below is written in the founder's public voice: clean English, no Hinglish, no dashes, long reasoned paragraphs where the copy argues a point, per voice.md and SYSTEM.md section 8. Every SEO title stays under sixty characters and every description under a hundred and sixty, standard practice for search result display.

Purpose: state what VEXA is and does in the buyer's own terms within the first screen, then route the visitor to the page that answers whichever of the six questions they came with.

SEO title: "VEXA Digital, Brand Partnerships for Gaming in India"
SEO description: "VEXA Digital finds a brand's way into gaming, teams, creators, tournaments or communities, and runs the partnership to a target agreed before any money moves."

This section exists to name the buyer's own objection back to them before proposing anything, the exact move voice.md records the founder making in a real brand meeting.

**What we actually do.** Three part structure, not a generic three card feature row (the taste skill's anti slop rule): a business problem the brand already has, gaming's actual relevance to that problem worked out honestly, and a campaign run to a number agreed in advance. Text: "We start from the brand's real business problem, not from a gaming trend. We work out honestly whether gaming can move that problem, and how. If it can, we design the campaign, choose the right partner, whether that is a team, a creator, a tournament, or a community, and run the whole thing to a target set jointly before launch. (The closing line is the exact register voice.md sets as the model line: "we can tell you honestly if this is not the right spend for you right now.")

**How we are paid, briefly.** One short paragraph, no specific rupee figure (see open question 1 below): "Our fee covers strategy, campaign design, choosing partners, coordinating everyone involved, and reporting on results. **Valuate strip.** A compact section, not a full page repeat: "We also built Valuate, a free tool that tells a creator honestly whether a brand deal is lowball, fair, or premium before they sign it." Links to `/valuate` and to valuate.vexadigital.in directly.

### 2.2 How We Work, `/how-we-work`

Purpose: answer "what do we do" in full, showing the actual method rather than agency language, which is the visual and written equivalent of opening the books.

SEO title: "How VEXA Works, Campaign Method and Process"
SEO description: "The order VEXA runs a campaign in: business problem, consumer behaviour, gaming relevance, mechanism, activation, measured outcome, decision, cost last."

**The real question.** Drawn directly from campaign-method.md's opening move: "A brand usually asks us how to sell more. That is not quite the right question. The real question is which moment is actually yours, because you cannot win on price and you cannot win on taste alone. You compete on moments." This is the single sharpest piece of doctrine language in the whole brain and belongs on the page verbatim in spirit.

**The order we work in.** Presented as a numbered sequence, not a diagram with framework names (campaign-method.md is explicit the deck "carries no framework names," a stakeholder needs the commercial consequence, not a lesson in the method): business problem, consumer behaviour, gaming relevance, the commercial mechanism, the activation itself, the measured outcome, the business decision it supports, cost last.

**How a target gets set.** "We do not wait for you to hand us a number. If you have a target, we test our plan against it. If you do not, we tell you what we believe the minimum target should be to hit the outcome you want, and we size the distribution spend to make that target realistically reachable. Either way, a pass and fail mark exists before launch, agreed jointly, so nothing gets judged later against a figure nobody agreed to." (Source: commercial-doctrine.md, the pass mark section.)

**What we will tell you no on.** One honest paragraph: "We have told a brand their product was not ready to compete in the segment they wanted and belonged somewhere else in the market instead. We have told a partner that brands sponsor what already exists, not what is still being built. We would rather lose a sale than sell you something we cannot defend later." (Source: commercial-doctrine.md, the Infinix and Piyush examples, kept unnamed here since the public line is the pattern, not the named party; confirm with Soda whether naming Infinix and Piyush publicly is acceptable, see open question 3.)

**What happens if a campaign is heading to miss its mark.** "If we see a campaign is tracking to miss its pass mark, we tell you the day we know it, recommend against going the full distance, and take two days to bring you a way to fix it." (Source: commercial-doctrine.md.)

CTA: "See what a first call looks like." Links to `/contact`.

### 2.7 Legal pages, `/legal/privacy`, `/legal/terms`, `/legal/grievance-officer`

These three pages are compliance surfaces, not brand copy, and none of their substantive legal content is invented here. This plan specifies the structure each page needs; the actual clauses need Soda's own drafting or a proper legal review pass, since a fabricated privacy policy or terms document creates real liability rather than reducing it.

**Privacy Policy**, structure only: what personal data the contact form and any analytics collect, why it is collected, how long it is kept, whether it is shared with any processor, and how a visitor can request deletion, consistent with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Gap: the actual data flows, retention periods, and any processor named need Soda's confirmation before this page can be written for real.

**Terms of Use**, structure only: what the site is (an informational and lead generation site, not a platform with user accounts at this stage, unless Valuate's account layer changes that), acceptable use, intellectual property notice for the VEXA and Valuate marks, and a limitation of liability. Gap: needs Soda's or a lawyer's actual drafting.

**Grievance Officer**, structure only: India's IT Rules, 2021 require a published grievance officer name, designation, contact email, and address for any entity handling user generated content or personal data at meaningful scale. Gap: VEXA has no grievance officer named anywhere in the brain files read for this plan. This is a genuine open question, not a copy decision, flagged below as open question 6.
